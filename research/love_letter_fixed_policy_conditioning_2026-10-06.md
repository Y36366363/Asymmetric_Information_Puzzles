# Love Letter fixed-policy conditioning audit — 2026-10-06

## Frozen question

The certified four-card late-round Love Letter subgame already separated its
combinatorial chance prior from opponent-policy reach. Under the exact
equilibrium, only 17 of 60 information sets have positive reach and none of
those 17 has a posterior different from its combinatorial prior.

Before inspecting new results, this update froze a deterministic, full-support
opponent policy. At every information set, legal actions receive raw weight:

`1 + 0.75 × kept card + 0.05 × played card + 0.02 × Guard guess`

and are then normalized. This is an experimental likelihood model, not a GTO
candidate. The preregistered primary endpoints were maximum L1 displacement of
posterior from prior and the count of information sets crossing `1e-6`.

## Result: valid zero conditioning signal

The policy gives positive reach to all 60 information sets, compared with the
equilibrium control's 17 positive-reach and 43 zero-reach sets. Nevertheless:

- maximum posterior L1 displacement is exactly zero;
- zero of 60 information sets crosses the `1e-6` threshold;
- Bayes reconstruction, action-value reconstruction and value additivity errors
  are all zero;
- the exact equilibrium control retains exploitability zero;
- the fixed policy has exploitability about `0.358319`, fails the `0.001`
  equilibrium gate and remains explicitly ineligible for a GTO label.

This is not a failed implementation. The mechanical audit passes. The
preregistered positive-signal hypothesis does not.

## Why the current-hand policy cannot shift this panel

Within each acting-player information set, the two remaining hidden-world
records differ in which of the opponent's two cards was initially dealt and
which was later drawn. The opponent's current two-card hand at their public
action is the same in both records. The frozen policy depends on the current
hand, played card and Guard guess, but not on the private acquisition order.
Consequently its action likelihood is identical in both hidden worlds. The
Bayesian product therefore multiplies the two prior masses by the same factor,
leaving the posterior unchanged.

This identifies an estimand mismatch: the current four-card hidden-world label
distinguishes private histories that a current-hand-only opponent model cannot
identify from action likelihoods. Increasing coefficients would not fix that
structural mismatch, so no post-hoc coefficient sweep is justified.

## Next mechanism decision

A later experiment has two defensible choices, each requiring a separate
preregistration:

1. change the fixed opponent policy to depend explicitly on private acquisition
   history, thereby testing history-sensitive opponent modeling; or
2. redefine the posterior target to collapse acquisition-order-equivalent worlds
   and test only strategically distinguishable hidden state.

The first is the cleaner manipulation check, while the second is the cleaner
representation. Neither should be silently substituted into today's experiment.
The exact four-card equilibrium remains certified, but the complete 16-card
Love Letter round remains uncertified and runtime ε-GTO remains forbidden.
